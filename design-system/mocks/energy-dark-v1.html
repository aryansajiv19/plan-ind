Dark as the identity — and the flattening solved, not accepted.

Dark is the identity and #051822 — the darker blue — is the canvas, as the owner asked. That leaves one question the palette does not answer on its own: how far should a card sit from the ground it lies on?

An earlier version of this page got that rule wrong, and the correction is the interesting part. It concluded that cards must sit below the canvas. What it had actually measured was that a ΔL* 15.54 step — the jump to #2D383E — leaves only one legible text colour inside a card. That is true. But the cause was the size of the step, not its direction, and stating it as a rule about direction was over-drawing from a single measurement.

Magnitude is the whole question, and it has two bounds. Below ΔL* 3.34 two surfaces read as one flat field — that is exactly why the near-white ground looked monotonous, and that finding still holds. Too far above it and the card runs out of headroom for text. #0B2836 sits at ΔL* 7.47: more than double the lower bound, and it keeps every ink the card needs — including --muted, the second line of every venue card, which dies at 4.24 on #2D383E.

This reverses two written directions, deliberately.

"Emphasise white heavily" — superseded 2026-09-07: the owner called the near-white ground monotonous. The measurement above says why it was always going to read that way.

"No dark mode, hold it back" — superseded 2026-09-07 by "I think I like the dark theme more." Worth noting this is a correction rather than drift: the six values were chosen before dark was ever asked for, and two of them are proper darks. Dark was latent in the palette the owner picked — it is not a theme bolted onto a light system.

The shipping blocker

The focus ring

§19.7's graphite inset ring is invisible on a near-black card — that is keyboard accessibility on the app's primary surface, so dark cannot ship without solving it. Tab through the buttons below (or click one) to see the ring on all four grounds a control can actually sit on.

Card #0B2836

Canvas #051822

Tan fill #AA7452

Light fill #D4C9C7

No single colour can do this job. Every candidate — white, light, grey, tan, brown, slate, navy, the derived accent — fails 3:1 on at least one of these four grounds. White fails on the light fill at 1.62; light fails on itself. So the ring is two bands: #D4C9C7 outer, #051822 inner, both owner values. On any ground at least one band clears the floor — worst case 4.59, on the tan fill. Under §24's grounds the outer band carries both dark surfaces (11.19 canvas, 9.47 card) and the inner band's job is now exclusively the light fills. Two tones are still required: no single value survives both ends. White would score better on the two dark grounds (18.10 / 12.02) but that contrast is already surplus, and it would buy it by shipping a value outside the six.

What I am proposing

  1. Dark is the identity, the owner's darker blue #051822 is the canvas, and cards sit ΔL* 7.47 away at #0B2836. That band is the whole answer to the flattening — every ink the card needs stays legible, including --muted, and the ground finally differs from its cards by more than a rounding error. Direction was never the rule; magnitude is.
  2. §21's accent is re-derived, not ported. Brown dies in dark (2.87 canvas, 1.91 card). The replacement is #BF977D — tan lightened 25% toward white — which clears body text on both dark surfaces (4.54 / 6.84), so §21 keeps its single rule instead of growing a per-surface exception. Tan and light stay as fills, unchanged. Grey finally earns a token as the boundary hairline, the job §19.1 said it had none for.
  3. Energy is scale, motion and density — never more colour. Every change in the three moments above is a font-size or a padding. The palette, §21's placement rule and its one-fill ceiling are untouched.
  4. Ungate the reveal from photography. It is the one genuinely joyful thing in the build and it has been invisible since the day it shipped.

What I did not do: put colour blocks back on cards, add an identity colour, or propose anything that needs photography to arrive. If the catalogue never gets past six photographs, all four of these still work.
