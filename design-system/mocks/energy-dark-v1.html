Dark as the identity — and the flattening solved, not accepted.

Making dark the standard raises one blocking problem: on #2D383E only #D4C9C7 clears the 4.5 floor, so card interiors go single-colour. That is the exact flattening the v6 palette was rejected for, and it is not shippable as a default.

It turned out to be self-inflicted. The dark mock made cards lighter than the canvas, copying light mode's raised-plane idea. That single choice burned 15.5 points of L* headroom — the exact band grey and tan live in. Put the cards below the canvas instead and the problem disappears with no new colour at all: four legible text tiers inside a card, which is one more than light mode has.

It also answers the background complaint. Today's canvas and cards differ by ΔL* 3.34 — near-white on white, which is precisely why the ground reads as one inert field. Dark, done this way, separates them by ΔL* 15.54. The canvas stops being a backdrop and becomes a material the cards are cut into.

This reverses two written directions, deliberately.

"Emphasise white heavily" — superseded 2026-09-07: the owner called the near-white ground monotonous. The measurement above says why it was always going to read that way.

"No dark mode, hold it back" — superseded 2026-09-07 by "I think I like the dark theme more." Worth noting this is a correction rather than drift: the six values were chosen before dark was ever asked for, and two of them are proper darks. Dark was latent in the palette the owner picked — it is not a theme bolted onto a light system.

Same component, same canvas, one substitution: drawImage becomes fillText. It then runs on every decision instead of 7% of them, and it can never taint the canvas, because the pixels are drawn locally rather than fetched from a third-party host — which deletes the fallback path the current version needs. When the winner does have a photo the photo version still wins; this is the other 92%. Light type assembling out of a near-black ground is a genuinely dramatic beat that the near-white ground could not have carried. Under prefers-reduced-motion the type simply renders, no particles.

What I am proposing

  1. Dark is the identity, and cards sit below the canvas. This is the whole answer to the flattening — no seventh colour, four text tiers instead of one, and a ground that finally differs from its cards by more than a rounding error.
  2. §21's accent is re-derived, not ported. Brown dies in dark (2.87 canvas, 1.91 card). The replacement is #BF977D — tan lightened 25% toward white — which clears body text on both dark surfaces (4.54 / 6.84), so §21 keeps its single rule instead of growing a per-surface exception. Tan and light stay as fills, unchanged. Grey finally earns a token as the boundary hairline, the job §19.1 said it had none for.
  3. Energy is scale, motion and density — never more colour. Every change in the three moments above is a font-size or a padding. The palette, §21's placement rule and its one-fill ceiling are untouched.
  4. Ungate the reveal from photography. It is the one genuinely joyful thing in the build and it has been invisible since the day it shipped.

What I did not do: put colour blocks back on cards, add an identity colour, or propose anything that needs photography to arrive. If the catalogue never gets past six photographs, all four of these still work.
