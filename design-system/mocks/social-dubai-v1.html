1 · The canvas earns its size. Warm light pooling in dark navy is what Dubai looks like at night, it needs no photograph, and it is the largest surface the app owns. Three radial-gradient layers drift over 96–152 seconds — if you notice it as motion it is too strong. Never filter: blur(): a blurred layer this size is the most expensive thing you can hand a mid-range Android. A gradient paints once into its own layer and then only translates, which is compositor work. The alphas are 0.10 / 0.08 / 0.06 and that is a measured ceiling, not a taste setting. Stack all three glows at full peak — which their positions make impossible, so it is the worst case rather than the expectation — and the canvas lightens to #2b2f30, where --muted still reads 4.77 against a 4.5 floor. At the alphas I first drew (0.14 / 0.11 / 0.09) that same stack gives 4.16 and fails. The atmosphere must never be able to eat the text, even in a configuration that cannot occur.

2 · The fun is the words, not a rainbow. Twenty-three categories set in Cormorant at three sizes reads as abundance — the thing a night out actually offers. I did not give each category a colour, and that is deliberate. §21 retired exactly that and the palette has six values for twenty-three categories, so "colour by category" can only end as the rainbow it already ended as once. The one fill goes to your choice — colour keyed to state, which is information, not decoration, and it keeps §21.5's one-per-screen ceiling intact. If the owner wants category colour after seeing this, that is a real conversation — but it needs a colour system we do not have, not a tweak.

The social answer: hold the space, don't hide it. Every member of the plan is drawn — the ones who have voted as faces, the ones who haven't as open seats. An empty seat is a person who hasn't answered yet, not an absence, and it is countable: you can see you are waiting on four people without reading a word.

This is what makes a single-player screen feel like a group. The alternative — rendering only the people who have acted — makes a plan with one vote look like a tool with one user. Same data, no new queries: the plan already knows its members. The seats are also where the voting avatars land (§25.3), so the empty seat is not decoration; it is the destination.

What this is and isn't

No new colour, no photographs, no invented content. The palette is frozen at §24 and untouched. Every category, name, area and price on this page is real data the app already has.

Nothing here needs a person to be online. The atmosphere is a still-life, the seats are the plan's own membership, and the categories are a table. It reads social because the interface expects company — not because it is faking any.

Mobile: transform and opacity only, three composited gradient layers, no blur, no filter, no JavaScript in the ambient path at all. Under prefers-reduced-motion the atmosphere stays and only the drift stops — nothing on this page carries information through movement, which is the test.
